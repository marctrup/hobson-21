What is lease management software?

Lease management software brings lease records, important dates and contractual obligations into one place. It helps a property team understand what has been agreed, what needs attention and which document supports the answer.

What does it actually do?

A lease is more than a file to store. It records parties, premises, rent, review mechanisms, repair responsibilities, break rights and conditions. Lease management software organises these details so they can be searched, reviewed and used in day-to-day work.

The scope varies by product. Some systems emphasise date reminders and reporting; others include financial administration, document extraction or lease accounting. Do not assume that a product described as lease management software includes all of these.

  • Keep lease documents and records together.
  • Record rent reviews, breaks and expiry dates with their conditions.
  • Find obligations and allocate follow-up work.
  • Report across leases rather than opening each file separately.

Lease management, property management and abstraction

Property management software has a broader operational remit: depending on the product, it may cover rent collection, maintenance, tenant communication and accounting. Lease management concentrates on the agreement and the obligations it creates.

Lease abstraction is a narrower task: extracting selected terms from a lease into a structured summary. It can supply the information used in a lease management system, but a summary alone does not manage the ongoing relationship.

Lease accounting is another distinct requirement. If you need IFRS 16 calculations or accounting outputs, ask for a demonstration of those functions specifically. Document extraction is not a substitute for an accounting engine.

A break date is not just a date

Imagine a team asking when a tenant can end a lease. A useful record needs more than the break date: it may need a notice period, a service method and conditions that affect whether the right can be exercised.

The team should check the original clause and any later documents before acting. A deed of variation may change the position. A system should make that evidence accessible, not encourage a decision based only on a calendar entry.

This example is illustrative, not legal advice. A qualified adviser should review legal interpretation and formal notices.

What to check in a demonstration

Use a representative lease from your own portfolio, with permission to share it. Include a later variation and a question whose answer you already know. This reveals more than a polished demonstration using a simple sample.

  • Can you trace a value back to the exact passage and page?
  • Can the system distinguish a missing fact from a confirmed answer?
  • How are variations and related documents connected?
  • Who reviews extracted information and approves actions?
  • Can you export records and supporting evidence when you leave?
  • What access controls, retention terms and support arrangements apply?

Does lease management software replace lease accounting?+
